DSP noise reduction between your radio and your speaker. It listens to the noise, learns what it sounds like, and removes it — while weak signals pass through. One box, three modes, no computer required.

How it works Set it up

The problem

HF noise — band static, QRM, the roar of a bad propagation night — sits on top of the very signals you are trying to hear. Turning up the volume turns up the noise with it. Noise reduction inside the radio helps, but the receiver is not the whole path.

What QRNix does

QRNix is a standalone box that processes the audio between your radio and your speaker or amplifier:

Radio line/headphone out→ QRNix→ Speaker / amplifier

It works with any radio — modern rig or vintage boat anchor — because it only needs the audio that would otherwise go to a speaker. Two modes of noise reduction cover the two situations every HF operator knows: a stable noise floor that never changes, and a band that shifts under you while you tune.

Everything is set with four knobs and one button. There is no software to install and no display to configure — the OLED shows what the box is doing, and a level indicator makes setup a one-time affair.

Four knobs, one button

Each knob controls one aspect of the suppression. In practice you set them once per band and forget them.

Reduction

How much of the remaining noise stays. Turn it up to push the noise down; turn it down to keep more of the background. 0–30 dB.

Smoothing

Steadies the output when the noise level wobbles. Too much smoothing can smear fast speech — use only as much as the band demands.

Whitening

Softens the tonal, hissy residue that aggressive suppression can leave behind, making it sound more like natural background.

Aggression

How hard the box works to remove noise. Low settings are transparent; high settings dig out weak signals from heavy noise, at the cost of some character in the audio.

The button has two jobs: a quick tap cycles the extra features (tone-kill and post-filter); holding it captures a noise profile in Spectral mode or clears the features in Bypass.

Three modes

A three-position switch selects the mode. Center is off — pure, untouched audio, useful as the reference for everything else.

Bypass center

The radio's audio passes through unchanged. Use it to compare, to A/B your settings, or when the band is quiet and you want zero processing in the path.

Spectral right

You capture the noise once — hold the button for a second during a quiet moment — and QRNix subtracts exactly that noise profile from everything that follows. Because the box knows precisely what the noise looks like, weak signals buried in stable band noise come through remarkably well.

Recapture whenever the noise character changes: after retuning, or when the band state shifts. The profile survives mode changes, so you can switch away and back without recapturing.

Adaptive left

No capture needed. The box continuously estimates the noise and updates its suppression as the band changes — ideal while tuning across the band or when the noise floor drifts. Best used when the noise is changing faster than you could recapture a profile.

Which mode when?

Stable noise, weak signals

Spectral. A captured profile is unambiguous: the box never confuses your noise floor with a weak voice. This is the mode for digging out the faint ones.

Changing conditions

Adaptive. Tuning, QRM that drifts, a band that fades — the estimator follows. Enter it during a quiet moment if you can; the first second seeds its baseline.

Just listening

Bypass. Nothing processed, nothing added. The honest reference for what the band actually sounds like.

Two extra features, one button

A quick tap of the button cycles through the feature combinations. The OLED shows what is armed; active features are shown inverted.

Tone-kill TK

Every HF operator knows the sound: another station tuning their transmitter, holding the key for seconds while matching pitch — a steady whistle right on the frequency. Tone-kill detects exactly that signature (a continuous tone in the 600–800 Hz range, held for over a second) and removes it completely, in any mode.

It cannot be fooled by real CW: no legitimate key-down lasts that long. Your own keying, beacons, and everything else pass untouched.

Post-filter PP

Aggressive suppression can leave a shimmering, "musical" residue on weak, noisy frames. The post-filter blurs that residue — the output sounds smoother and more natural when the going gets rough. It works in the two noise-reduction modes and does nothing in Bypass.

CLIP — level indicator

If the signal into the box is too hot, the audio clips — and clipping sounds like distortion, not noise. QRNix detects saturation in the digital stream and flashes the top line of the display. Turn the radio's output down (or use the input trim) until the flash stops: that is your level, set once.

Auto input selection

Both line inputs are monitored. QRNix processes the left input by default and automatically switches to the right when it is significantly stronger — handy when a cable or adapter feeds the "wrong" channel.

In the shack

What you need

  • Your radio — any radio with a line, headphone, or speaker output
  • A speaker or amplifier — the box sits between the radio and it
  • One stereo audio cable (radio → QRNix) and one (QRNix → amplifier)
  • USB power — any phone charger or USB port

Connecting

  1. Connect the radio's line out (or headphone out) to QRNix's line in. Speaker outputs work too — QRNix handles the level with its input trim.
  2. Connect QRNix's line out to your speaker or amplifier.
  3. Plug in USB power. The display lights up with the QRNix boot screen.
  4. Start with the mode switch in the center (Bypass) and all controls at their minimums. You should hear your radio, unprocessed.
  5. Set the level: transmit-adjacent peaks or band noise should not flash the top display line (CLIP). Adjust the radio's output or the input trim until it stops.
  6. Turn the mode switch to Spectral or Adaptive and raise the controls until the noise drops and the signals stay.
